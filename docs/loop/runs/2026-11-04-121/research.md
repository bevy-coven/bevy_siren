# Iteration 121 — research: seeding a linear generator

**The question.** `model::Rng::new(seed)` is xorshift64 whose state *is* the seed. Measured on the unchanged tree (`evidence/before/seed-probe-on-the-unchanged-tree.txt`): the first pick of a cue is its first variant for **3000 of 3000** seeds; the first draws of neighbouring seeds correlate at **r = 0.9995**; one flipped seed bit changes **3.8 of 32** bits of the first draw
(bits 0 and 1: none); and the stream of seed `a ⊕ b` is the stream of `a` xor the stream of `b` **for all of the first 1000 draws, in 1953 of 1953 triples**. What does the literature say about seeding such a generator, which fix is the smallest that cures it, and how is the cure held by a test?

## The technique, in the sources' terms

1. **Do not seed a linear generator linearly** (Brent §4): put a **non-F₂-linear** step between the seed and the state. Modular addition of a Weyl increment is such a step; a bijective bit-mixer after it (SplitMix64's finalizer) gives **full avalanche**, so that no low-weight or xor-related seed survives as a low-weight or xor-related state.
2. **Keep the generator.** The remedy is at the seed. The generator's other properties are untouched, and the documentation's *"deterministic xorshift64"* stays true.
3. **The state must not be zero** (Marsaglia; the crate's own doc): the finalizer is a bijection with 0 ↦ 0, so **exactly one seed — `−γ mod 2⁶⁴ = 0x61C8864680B583EB` — maps to the zero state**. It needs its own guard (an independent check shows `splitmix64(0x61C8864680B583EB) = 0`).
4. **Hold the property, not the number**: a seed must open a stream the way a *random* seed would. Four checks, each of which the unchanged tree fails by orders of magnitude: **uniformity** of what a seed opens with (chi-square), **serial correlation** across neighbouring seeds (Pearson r), **avalanche** (Hamming distance of the first draw under a one-bit change of seed) and **no xor-linearity** between related seeds' streams. And **known answers** for the mixing function and the stream, from an independent implementation, so the constants cannot drift unseen.

## What good looks like

There is no spectrogram for this one: **a seed is good when it opens a stream the way a random seed would**, and what a player hears is the consequence. A game seeded with 1, 2 and 3 should open on different variants in the proportions the variant count says (a third each for three variants) and with detunes spread over the authored range; two seeds should not share an opening; and the neighbouring seeds' streams should be uncorrelated from the first draw (|r| < 0.073 at every n). The first draws should vary with **every** bit of the seed (a mean flip of 16 of 32). The same seed must still replay the same sounds, field for field, in any process.
