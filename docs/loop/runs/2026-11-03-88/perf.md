# Iteration 88 — perf

## Indexing throughput — `examples/indexbench`

Apple M4, `--release`, idle. A 0.5 s mono 48 kHz WAV corpus generated by the bench itself, so the figure is reproducible.

| corpus | decode | total (decode + synthetic embed) |
|---|---|---|
| 200 clips | **2 411 clips/s** (0.415 ms/clip) | **1 856 clips/s** (0.539 ms/clip) |
| 500 clips | **2 636 clips/s** (0.379 ms/clip) | **1 812 clips/s** (0.552 ms/clip) |
| 1000 clips | **2 724 clips/s** (0.367 ms/clip) | **1 846 clips/s** (0.542 ms/clip) |

**Stable across three sizes to within 10 %**, which is what makes the 5 000-clip projection meaningful:

| library | at the measured rate |
|---|---|
| 500 clips | **0.3 s** |
| 5 000 clips | **3 s** |

**`decode` includes the loudness measurement**, because `embed_measured` measures on the decode side of the boundary.
So the cost iterations 85–86 added is *inside* this number, not missing from it.

**The bound is decode**, at these rates: decode 0.37–0.42 ms/clip against a synthetic embed of 0.12–0.16 ms/clip. But
that synthetic embed is **262 144 multiply-adds, not the model** — a CLAP audio tower on a CPU measured **34.9 ms per
clip** in ADR 0002. At that figure inference would dominate by ~100×, so **the real bound on a CPU is inference**, and
the bench's honest claim is the pipeline shape plus the decode number, not the bound.
